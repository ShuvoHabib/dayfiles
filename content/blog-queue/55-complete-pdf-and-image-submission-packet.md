How do you complete a submission packet when the materials include both PDFs and images? Treat the job as one delivery workflow, not as separate random edits. Photos, scans, forms, and final PDFs should move through a clear order before the packet is uploaded or emailed.

## Why mixed packets need structure

Mixed packets are risky because each file type has different failure points. Images may be too large, in the wrong format, or missing privacy cleanup. PDFs may have wrong page order, blank scans, missing signatures, or file-size problems. Fixing these at the end creates rework.

Use [Images Dayfiles](https://images.dayfiles.com/) for image preparation and [PDF Dayfiles](https://dayfiles.com/) or the [Android app](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.dayfiles.pdfprocessor) for PDF assembly and review.

## A complete packet sequence

1. Collect all source files.
2. Separate images from PDFs.
3. Convert, resize, or compress images first.
4. Blur or remove sensitive image details if needed.
5. Convert images to PDF pages when the packet requires it.
6. Merge or organize the PDF packet.
7. Compress the final PDF only if necessary.
8. Open the final packet and review every page.
9. Archive the source folder and delivery copy.

This order prevents image problems from appearing after the PDF is already assembled.

## What should the final review catch?

Check destination rules, file size, page count, naming, signatures, image readability, page order, and whether any source material that should have stayed internal is still present. The packet should match the audience, not just the folder contents.

## Final takeaway

Submission packets work best when images are cleaned before PDF assembly and the final PDF is reviewed as one complete delivery file. Fix the sources, build the packet, then inspect the result before it leaves.
